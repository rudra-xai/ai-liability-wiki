The **EU Artificial Intelligence Act** is the European Union's horizontal regulation of AI systems. It sorts AI uses into risk tiers: prohibited practices, high-risk systems subject to conformity obligations, transparency duties for certain systems, and separate obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models.[^oj]

## Structure

- **Prohibited practices**, such as certain social scoring and manipulative techniques, have applied since February 2, 2025.
- **General-purpose AI models** face documentation, copyright-policy and, for systemic-risk models, evaluation and incident-reporting duties from August 2, 2025.
- **High-risk systems** listed in Annex III must meet requirements on risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight, accuracy and robustness. The Commission proposed in November 2025 to adjust the timetable for some of these obligations; check current status before relying on a date.[^oj]

## Insurance-specific provisions

Annex III lists as high-risk AI systems intended to be used for **risk assessment and pricing for natural persons in life and health insurance**. Insurers deploying such systems take on deployer obligations, including human oversight and, for some deployers, a fundamental-rights impact assessment.[^oj]

## Liability

The AI Act is a regulatory statute; it does not itself create a general civil-liability regime. The Commission withdrew the proposed [[eu-ai-liability-directive|AI Liability Directive]] in 2025, so compensation claims in the EU rely on national tort law and the revised [[eu-product-liability-directive|Product Liability Directive]], which expressly covers software.
