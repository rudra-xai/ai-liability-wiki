The revised **Product Liability Directive** modernizes the EU's 1985 strict-liability regime for defective products. For AI liability its central change is that **software is a product**, whether embedded or standalone, so AI system providers can face no-fault liability for damage caused by defects.[^pld]

## Key changes

- **Software and AI** are expressly products; open-source software developed outside a commercial activity is excluded.
- **Defectiveness** takes account of a product's ability to keep learning after deployment and of cybersecurity requirements.
- **Disclosure and presumptions.** Courts can order defendants to disclose evidence, and defect or causation can be presumed where technical complexity makes proof excessively difficult.
- **Damage** now includes destruction or corruption of data and medically recognized psychological harm.[^pld]

## Why it matters for insurers

Strict liability for AI software in the EU's largest markets will flow into product liability and technology E&O pricing from late 2026. Because the separate [[eu-ai-liability-directive|AI Liability Directive]] was withdrawn, this directive is now the main EU-level route for AI damage claims.

[^pld]: [Directive (EU) 2024/2853 on liability for defective products](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj), Official Journal, Nov. 18, 2024.
