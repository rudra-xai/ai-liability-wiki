The proposed **AI Liability Directive** would have harmonized parts of national fault-based liability for AI-caused damage, mainly through disclosure orders and a rebuttable presumption of causation where a defendant breached a relevant duty of care.[^prop]

## Withdrawal

The European Commission listed the proposal for withdrawal in its February 2025 work programme, citing no foreseeable agreement among the co-legislators. Members of the European Parliament criticized the decision, and civil-liability harmonization remains under debate.[^prop]

## What fills the gap

Victims of AI-caused harm in the EU now rely on national tort law and the revised [[eu-product-liability-directive|Product Liability Directive]]. See also [[ai-liability-regimes]] for the wider strict-versus-fault debate.

[^prop]: [Proposal for an AI Liability Directive, COM(2022) 496](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52022PC0496).
