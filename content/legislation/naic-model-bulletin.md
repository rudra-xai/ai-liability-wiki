The **NAIC Model Bulletin on the Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers** sets regulators' expectations for how insurers govern AI. It does not create new law; it reminds insurers that existing unfair-trade-practice, unfair-discrimination and claims-handling laws apply to AI-supported decisions, and describes the governance regulators expect to see.[^naic]

## Expectations

- A written **AI systems program** proportionate to risk, covering governance, risk management and internal controls across the AI life cycle.
- Controls on **data quality, bias, drift and explainability** for systems used in underwriting, pricing, claims and fraud detection.
- Oversight of **third-party AI vendors**, including audit and cooperation rights in contracts.
- Documentation that regulators can request during market-conduct examinations.[^naic]

## Adoption

The bulletin takes effect state by state as each insurance department adopts it, sometimes with modifications. A majority of states have now done so; a few, such as New York and Colorado, rely on their own rules. See [[ny-dfs-circular-letter-7]] and [[colorado-ai-act]].

## Update, 30 October 2026

The NAIC working group exposed version 6.0 of its AI Risk Evaluation Supplement, with comments due 30 October, and North Dakota issued Bulletin 2026-2 encouraging a written AI program.[^iaw]
