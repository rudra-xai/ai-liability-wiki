Character Technologies, Inc., the developer of [[character-ai|Character.AI]], filed for Chapter 11 protection in Delaware on October 12, 2026. According to a Reuters report carried by Insurance Journal, the company aims to use the case to resolve state investigations and lawsuits, license technology and intellectual property to Disney for $20 million (subject to court approval and higher offers), and find a buyer for its chatbot business.[^ij]

## State attorneys general

On October 14–15, 2026 a bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general filed a notice in the case. California's attorney general described the filing as asking the court to address privacy and safety concerns, and as reserving the states' right to challenge any proposed sale or plan. The states' concerns include that the company's voluntary limits on minors' access to some chatbot features are not mandatory and could be reversed by a buyer.[^ca] At the first hearing the states' lawyer said settlement talks were under way over allegations, not adjudicated, that the chatbots impersonated licensed professionals and exposed children to abusive content.[^ij]

## Why it matters for insurers

- The case tests whether bankruptcy can bundle regulator claims and private chatbot-harm claims into one resolution. See [[chatbot-product-liability]].
- Reported preliminary settlements with families follow the dismissal of [[garcia-v-character-technologies]].
