*In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation* is a multidistrict litigation consolidating copyright suits by authors and news organizations, including *The New York Times*, against OpenAI and Microsoft. Plaintiffs allege that training and running large language models on their works without permission infringes copyright; the defendants rely principally on fair use.

## October 2026 stay orders

In mid-October 2026, Judge Sidney H. Stein stayed the Times Publishing action pending summary-judgment rulings in other active cases in the MDL. He ordered the USA Today plaintiffs to explain by October 30 why their case should not also be stayed, with any defense response due November 6.[^stay1][^stay2]

## USA Today publishers' complaint

According to a trade report, USA Today Co. and affiliates, publishers of 19 newspapers, sued seven OpenAI entities in the Southern District of New York on October 8, 2026, alleging copyright infringement and DMCA violations and seeking more than $250 million, an injunction and destruction of models and training datasets containing their material. The plaintiffs asked that the case be treated as related to this MDL. These are allegations; no OpenAI response was reported.[^usat]

## Why it matters for insurers

Training-data copyright claims are among the largest potential AI liabilities. Media and IP policies frequently exclude or sublimit them, and underwriters increasingly ask about training-data provenance. See [[ai-exclusions-and-policy-wording]].
