*Johnson v. Dunn* is a prisoner civil-rights case in which outside counsel defending Alabama prison officials filed motions containing case citations fabricated by a generative-AI tool. In July 2025 the court sanctioned the responsible lawyers.[^order]

## The sanctions

Judge Anna M. Manasco found that fabricated citations are a serious misrepresentation to the court regardless of intent. She publicly reprimanded the lawyers, disqualified them from further participation in the case, directed that the order be shared with their clients and with other courts where they appear, and referred the matter to licensing authorities.[^order] The court noted that the firm had internal AI policies, but held that policies do not replace each lawyer's duty to verify what they file.

## Why it matters for insurers

The order shows that AI-citation risk is not confined to solo practitioners. For lawyers' professional liability underwriting, it shifts the question from whether a firm *has* an AI policy to whether verification is actually enforced. See [[lawyers-professional-liability-and-ai]].

[^order]: Memorandum opinion and order on sanctions, *Johnson v. Dunn* (N.D. Ala. July 2025).
