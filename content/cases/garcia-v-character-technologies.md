*Garcia v. Character Technologies, Inc.* is a federal wrongful-death and product-liability action over the Character.AI companion chatbot. Megan Garcia alleged that the chatbot's design and interactions contributed to the death of her 14-year-old son. The allegations were never tested at trial: the parties settled, and on October 15, 2026 the plaintiffs filed notice dismissing the case with prejudice against Character Technologies, its two founders and Google.[^notice]

## Allegations

The amended complaint alleged defects including inadequate age verification, anthropomorphic design and insufficient user controls, and pleaded product-liability, negligence, failure-to-warn, wrongful-death, deceptive-practices and unjust-enrichment theories. It also alleged that Google contributed technology and infrastructure to Character.AI.[^order]

## May 2025 dismissal ruling

In May 2025, Judge Anne C. Conway granted the motions to dismiss in part and denied them in part, letting most claims proceed. Two holdings drew wide attention:[^order]

- **Product status.** The court treated Character.AI as a product for claims arising from alleged defects in the app's design and functionality, as distinct from the ideas expressed in its output. It did not adopt a rule that all software or all AI output is a product.
- **First Amendment.** At the pleading stage, the court declined to hold that the chatbot's output was protected speech barring the claims, while allowing the company to assert its users' rights as listeners.

These were pleading-sufficiency rulings, not findings of defect, causation or damages.

## Settlement and dismissal

The court was told in January 2026 that the case had settled. The October 2026 notice states that the settlement agreement was executed and any required court approvals obtained. Terms have not been disclosed.[^notice][^wkmg]

## Why it matters for insurers

- The case is the leading pleading-stage authority for treating a generative-AI app as a "product", which brings strict-liability theories, and product-liability insurance, into play for AI developers. See [[chatbot-product-liability]].
- Because it settled, there is no merits ruling on causation or on Google's liability as a component supplier.
- Companion-chatbot claims involving minors raise questions under bodily-injury triggers in general liability policies and under new generative-AI exclusions. See [[ai-exclusions-and-policy-wording]].
