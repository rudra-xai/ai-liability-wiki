*Citizens Insurance Co. of America v. Wynndalco Enterprises, LLC* is a coverage dispute arising from the litigation over Clearview AI's facial-recognition database. Wynndalco, which licensed Clearview's product to customers, was sued under the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) and sought a defense under its general liability policy.[^op]

## The coverage question

The policies covered "personal and advertising injury", including publication of material that violates a person's right of privacy, but excluded injury arising from violations of certain listed statutes plus a broad catch-all for violations of any other law addressing the collection or use of information. Citizens argued that the catch-all excluded BIPA claims.[^op]

## Holding

The Seventh Circuit held that the catch-all, read alongside the specific statutes listed before it, was ambiguous about whether it reached BIPA. Under Illinois law, ambiguity in an exclusion is construed against the insurer, so Citizens owed Wynndalco a defense.[^op]

## Why it matters

- It shows how claims against businesses in an **AI product's distribution chain**, not just its developer, can reach general liability policies written without AI in mind (so-called "silent AI").
- It helped push insurers toward express biometric and AI exclusions. See [[ai-exclusions-and-policy-wording]].
