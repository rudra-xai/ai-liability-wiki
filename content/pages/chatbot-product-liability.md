Whether generative-AI applications are **products**, and so subject to strict product liability, or **services and speech**, subject mainly to negligence and constitutional limits, is one of the central questions in AI liability.

## U.S. litigation

- [[garcia-v-character-technologies]]: a federal court treated Character.AI as a product for design-defect claims at the pleading stage in 2025; the case later settled.
- In October 2026, four California suits alleged that ChatGPT caused serious personal harm, and a separate proposed class action challenged the handling of children's data. These are allegations, not findings.
- Courts have distinguished alleged defects in an app's design, such as missing age verification, from the content of its output.

## European Union

The revised [[eu-product-liability-directive]] expressly treats software, including AI systems, as a product from December 2026, settling the question for EU product-liability claims.

## Insurance angle

Product status brings strict-liability theories into play, raising the importance of products-completed-operations coverage, technology E&O and the scope of generative-AI exclusions. See [[ai-exclusions-and-policy-wording]].
