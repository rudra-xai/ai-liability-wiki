As AI claims grow, insurers are moving from silence to explicit wording. Some add exclusions that remove AI-related loss from traditional lines; others add affirmative grants or endorsements that buy the exposure back. This page explains what to look for.

## Kinds of AI wording

- **Absolute generative-AI exclusions** remove loss "arising out of" generative AI. Wide "arising out of" language can reach claims where AI played only a minor role.
- **Narrow exclusions** target specific outputs, such as AI-generated content that infringes intellectual property, or exclude only the insured's own AI products.
- **Affirmative grants and endorsements** expressly cover defined AI events, sometimes with a sublimit or added warranties about human review.
- **Standard-form developments.** Verisk's ISO reportedly filed generative-AI exclusion endorsements for commercial general liability forms in 2025. Check whether a carrier has attached them at renewal.

## Questions to ask about an issued policy

1. How does the policy define "artificial intelligence" or "generative AI", and could routine software be caught?
2. Does an exclusion use "arising out of", "based upon" or "caused by"? These carry different causal tests.
3. Is there an exception for professional services supervised by a qualified human?
4. Do warranties or application answers about AI use risk a rescission argument if inaccurate?
5. Does the exclusion apply to defense costs, or only to indemnity?

## Disputes to watch

Few coverage decisions have yet construed an express AI exclusion. Earlier disputes over biometric and data exclusions, such as [[citizens-insurance-v-wynndalco]], show courts reading catch-all language narrowly where it is ambiguous.
