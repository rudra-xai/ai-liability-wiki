Alongside exclusions in traditional lines, a specialist market has formed that **expressly** insures AI risk. Products differ widely, but most fall into two groups.

## Performance guarantees

These policies or warranties pay when an AI model fails to meet agreed performance metrics, such as error rates for a classification model. Munich Re has offered AI performance insurance under its aiSure brand since 2018. Others back vendor warranties so that AI providers can offer customers contractual guarantees.

## AI liability cover

Newer products, some written with Lloyd's capacity, cover third-party claims arising from AI errors, such as hallucinations, discriminatory outcomes or harmful outputs, including for businesses that deploy AI rather than build it. Sector-specific offerings target professions where AI errors cause direct client harm, such as law. See [[lawyers-professional-liability-and-ai]].

## How it is underwritten

Underwriters typically assess the model's testing and evaluation record, monitoring and drift controls, human-review processes, incident history and governance frameworks such as the [[nist-ai-rmf]] or [[iso-iec-42001]]. Because many firms rely on the same few foundation models, **correlated loss** across policyholders is a central pricing concern.
