Insurers are among the heaviest users of predictive models, and their own AI use is a growing source of liability and regulation.

## Litigation

Challenges have centered on claim denials allegedly driven by algorithms, especially in health coverage. [[estate-of-lokken-v-unitedhealth]] alleges that an algorithm, [[nh-predict|nH Predict]], replaced individual clinical judgment in post-acute-care decisions; contract-based claims survived a 2025 motion to dismiss. Similar suits have targeted other health insurers' automated review processes.

## Supervisory rules

- **United States**: the [[naic-model-bulletin]] sets governance expectations adopted by most states; [[ny-dfs-circular-letter-7]] adds testing and notice duties in New York; Colorado regulates algorithmic discrimination by insurers under SB 21-169 and the [[colorado-ai-act]].
- **European Union**: the [[eu-ai-act]] classes AI for life and health insurance risk assessment and pricing as high-risk.

## Controls regulators look for

Inventory of AI systems; documented human review of adverse decisions; bias and drift testing; explainable reasons for consumers; and contractual audit rights over vendors.
