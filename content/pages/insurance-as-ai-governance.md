Insurers can shape AI behavior without legislation: they price risk, demand controls as a condition of cover, and share loss data. Scholars including [[anat-lior]] have argued that insurance can work as a governance tool for AI, much as it has for workplace safety and cybersecurity.

## Mechanisms

- **Underwriting questions** that reward testing, monitoring and human oversight.
- **Premium differentiation** for firms that adopt frameworks such as the [[nist-ai-rmf]].
- **Exclusions and warranties** that make coverage conditional on specific controls.
- **Claims data** that, over time, reveal which practices actually reduce loss.

## Limits

Insurance works best for frequent, independent and measurable losses. Catastrophic or highly correlated AI risks, such as a failure in a widely used foundation model, may exceed private market capacity. Silent-AI ambiguity also weakens price signals until wording is clarified.
